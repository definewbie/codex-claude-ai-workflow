types of LLMs I suggest using. One important paradigm here is the handoff.
A handoff defines how control shifts between agents: who speaks next, who
acts next, and under what conditions. In LangGraph, nodes encode the logic
of the agents, and edges represent the handoffs. By wiring multiple nodes
together, you’re effectively building a MAS within a single graph. The
shared state is the memory and context that all nodes operate on. How this
wiring looks in practice for ToT is illustrated in Example 2-6.

Example 2-6. Creating the graph for ToT
graph = StateGraph(BlogState)
graph.add_node("propose", propose_options)
graph.add_node("reflect", reflect_and_select)
graph.add_node("research", research_with_tools)
graph.add_node("draft", draft_outline_and_intro)

graph.add_edge(START, "propose")
graph.add_edge("propose", "reflect")
graph.add_edge("reflect", "research")
graph.add_edge("research", "draft")
graph.add_edge("draft", END)

checkpointer = MemorySaver()
app = graph.compile(checkpointer=checkpointer)
